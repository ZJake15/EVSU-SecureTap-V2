## Theoretical Framework

The proposed study, EVSU SecureTap: NFC and Facial Recognition-Based Campus Entry Monitoring System, is anchored on Access Control Theory and Biometric Authentication Theory. These theories provide the foundation for the development of a secure, reliable, and efficient campus access management system that authenticates users before granting access to university premises.

### Access Control Theory

Access Control Theory is based on the principle that access to a protected resource or location should only be granted to authorized individuals according to established security policies. It focuses on identifying users, authenticating their identities, authorizing access privileges, and maintaining records of access activities. The theory emphasizes confidentiality, integrity, and accountability by ensuring that only legitimate users can gain entry while unauthorized access is prevented.

In the context of this study, Access Control Theory serves as the primary foundation for the proposed system. The EVSU SecureTap system verifies the identity of students, faculty, staff, and other authorized personnel before allowing entry into the campus. By implementing access policies through NFC technology and facial recognition, the system enhances campus security, automates entry and exit monitoring, and maintains accurate records of access transactions in a centralized database.

### Biometric Authentication Theory

Biometric Authentication Theory states that an individual's identity can be verified using unique physiological or behavioral characteristics that are difficult to duplicate or forge. Unlike traditional authentication methods such as passwords or identification cards, biometric authentication relies on distinctive human traits, including facial features, fingerprints, iris patterns, and voice characteristics, to establish identity with a higher level of accuracy and security.

This theory supports the facial recognition component of the proposed system. After an NFC-enabled identification card is presented, the system performs facial recognition to verify that the person using the card is its legitimate owner. By combining NFC authentication with biometric verification, the proposed system strengthens security, minimizes identity fraud and card sharing, and improves the reliability of campus access control. The integration of these authentication methods ensures that only authorized individuals are granted access while maintaining efficient and accurate monitoring of campus entry and exit activities.

### Deep Learning Models for Face Recognition

Deep learning has transformed the field of facial recognition by significantly improving the accuracy, efficiency, and reliability of biometric identification systems. Unlike traditional machine learning methods that rely on manually extracted facial features, deep learning algorithms automatically learn complex facial representations from large datasets through multiple layers of neural networks. This capability enables facial recognition systems to accurately identify individuals despite variations in lighting conditions, facial expressions, camera angles, aging, and partial facial occlusions. As a result, deep learning has become the preferred approach for developing intelligent biometric systems used in security, surveillance, healthcare, banking, and educational institutions.

Among the various deep learning architectures, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have demonstrated exceptional performance in image recognition and facial identification tasks. CNN-based models such as ResNet-50, VGG-16, FaceNet, and InceptionResNet have been widely adopted because of their ability to extract high-level facial features while maintaining excellent recognition accuracy. These pre-trained models can identify subtle facial characteristics that distinguish one individual from another, making them highly suitable for automated attendance systems, campus access control, and intelligent surveillance applications. Furthermore, advances in transfer learning have enabled developers to build highly accurate facial recognition systems without requiring extremely large training datasets, reducing development time while maintaining high performance.

A 2025 study published in Procedia Computer Science evaluated the effectiveness of deep learning models for CCTV-based attendance systems by comparing the performance of ResNet-50 and VGG-16 in facial recognition tasks. The researchers implemented both convolutional neural network models using surveillance camera footage to automatically identify individuals and record attendance. The evaluation demonstrated that ResNet-50 achieved approximately 96% precision and 93% recall, outperforming VGG-16 in recognition accuracy, processing efficiency, and overall reliability. The researchers concluded that deeper neural network architectures are more effective in extracting complex facial features, resulting in higher recognition performance even when images contain variations in lighting, viewing angles, and facial expressions.

The study also highlighted the importance of integrating deep learning models with real-time monitoring systems and centralized databases. By combining CCTV cameras, artificial intelligence, and cloud-based databases, attendance records could be generated automatically and synchronized in real time without requiring manual intervention. Additionally, they noted that deep learning-based facial recognition systems can be continuously improved through additional training data and model optimization, making them adaptable to changing operational environments.
